Dashed line: the polyp as drawn by a pathologist. Route named right on 7 of 7 slices; changes repeat in a second group of people (97–99%).

Now the genes themselves, in single cells and in tissue side by side. In single cells, comparing each polyp with its own person’s lining, conventional polyps switch on Wnt genes, the growth signal of stem cells; serrated polyps switch on stomach-like genes; and every polyp switches off genes of mature cells. 97 to 99 percent of these changes point the same way in a second, separate group of people. Below are the same two tissue slices from the start, with each dot coloured by those textbook genes. On the serrated slice, stomach-like genes fill both polyp areas and the healthy lining between stays pale. On the conventional slice, Wnt genes mark patches of the polyp, and one patch of healthy lining too, because healthy stem cells use Wnt as well. Across all seven slices, these genes name the right route every time. The exact polyp border is harder: dot by dot, the genes agree with the pathologist about two times in three. (Single cells: paired DESeq2 per route, DIS 53 conventional-only and 68 serrated-only genes, Hallmark Wnt up in conventional in both cohorts; replication 98.9 / 97.4 percent same sign. Tissue: Visium 3' v1, fresh frozen; Wnt set AXIN2, LGR5, ASCL2, NKD1, SOX9, MYC; stomach-like set MUC5AC, TFF1, TFF2, ANXA10, AQP5, CLDN18; averaged with the 6 nearest spots for the picture; spot agreement 53 to 73 percent.)

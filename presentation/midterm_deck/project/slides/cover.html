Hi, we are Yuqi and Muhammed. Our question: a polyp in the colon can head toward cancer by two different routes. What decides which route it takes? On the right you see the two kinds as a doctor sees them during a colonoscopy: a conventional polyp, raised on a short stalk, and a serrated polyp, smaller and paler. And this drawing is one tube of the colon lining: stem cells sit at the bottom and make every other cell, which mature as they move up. Keep this picture in mind, because the two routes seem to start in different parts of this tube. Our idea is to read each polyp against the same person’s own healthy colon.

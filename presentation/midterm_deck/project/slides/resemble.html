Textbook: conventional starts in stem cells, serrated in mature cells.

Where do the new cells come from? We matched every polyp cell to the most similar healthy cells of the same person. Adenoma-type cells match best to stem cells in 11 of 14 people; serrated-type cells match best to mature cells in 9 of 10. The plot on the left checks the same thing without any matching, just from the cells’ own genes: one dot is one person’s cells of one kind. Adenoma-type cells, in blue, sit with or beyond healthy stem cells on stem-cell genes, in all 11 people who have both. Serrated-type cells, in orange, have few stem-cell genes and sit midway on mature-cell genes. Both agree with the textbook picture of Chen and colleagues: conventional polyps start in stem cells, serrated polyps in mature cells. Here we see it inside each person. (Matching: unbalanced optimal transport in a 30-PC space, same person only; ordinary polyp cells find their own healthy type 81 to 94 percent of the time; new cells are the hardest to match in 23 of 24 cases; same answer under 5 settings. Mature = mature absorbing cells. Genes: stem LGR5, OLFM4, SMOC2, ASCL2; mature CA1, SLC26A3, AQP8, CEACAM7. The atlas partly defined adenoma-type cells by stem-like genes, so the blue result is a consistency check.)

It proves we can compare each polyp with its own person

Why compare with the same person? This is the map of everyone’s cells, with two people lit up. In gray are that person’s healthy cells: they spread across the healthy area, mixed in with everyone else’s. In colour are that person’s polyp cells: they form their own island, right next to that person’s healthy cells, and other people’s polyps sit elsewhere. Then we asked directly: out of about twenty people’s healthy lining, which one does each polyp look most like? In 21 of 36 polyps it is its own person’s lining; by chance we would expect about two. So a polyp still carries its person’s fingerprint, and comparing it with other people would mix that fingerprint into the polyp signal. Comparing with its own person removes it, and makes our estimates about 16 percent tighter. The explorer link lets you pick any person live.

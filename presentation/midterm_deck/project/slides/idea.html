Our idea: read each polyp against the same person’s healthy lining. Every person starts from a different baseline, with different inherited genes, age, diet and gut bacteria. So we do four things. One, compare each polyp only with its own person’s healthy lining, so the background cancels out. Two, split the change into what every polyp does and what only one route does, counting how many people agree. Three, match each polyp cell to its most similar healthy cell from the same person, to see which healthy cell the polyp’s new cells look like. Four, take the genes that single cells point to and check them in the tissue itself: on a tissue slice, do they light up where the pathologist drew the polyp? Single cells tell us which genes to look for; the tissue tells us where they are. Then we ask: with these simple steps, do we already see the two routes the textbook describes, and can we see differences between individual people? (Step 3: unbalanced optimal transport inside each person; resemblance, not ancestry. Tissue: 10x Visium slices of 7 polyps; all 6 people behind them also have single cells.)

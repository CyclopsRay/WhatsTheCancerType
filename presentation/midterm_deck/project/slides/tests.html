Before the results, how we ran the first tests. Each test pairs a simple measurement with what the textbook expects. One, new cell types: we count the atlas’s polyp-only cell types in each polyp and in the same person’s lining; the textbook expects one new type per route. Two, where they start: we match the new cells to the same person’s healthy cells, and we also read their own stem-cell genes and mature-cell genes; the textbook says conventional polyps start in stem cells and serrated polyps in mature cells. Three, route genes: Wnt growth genes, the signal that keeps stem cells dividing, should rise in conventional polyps; stomach-like genes should rise in serrated polyps. Four, the tissue: the same two gene sets, on seven tissue slices, should mark their own polyp. The gene sets come from the textbook and were fixed before we looked at the result, and the score is simply how many people or slices agree. (Textbook: Fearon and Vogelstein 1990; Snover 2011; Chen et al. 2021 for the cell types and the stem versus differentiated origins. Full sets: stem LGR5, OLFM4, SMOC2, ASCL2; mature CA1, SLC26A3, AQP8, CEACAM7; Wnt AXIN2, LGR5, ASCL2, NKD1, SOX9, MYC; stomach-like MUC5AC, TFF1, TFF2, ANXA10, AQP5, CLDN18. Single-cell route genes also come from a genome-wide paired comparison, DESeq2, which picked up Wnt targets and stomach-like genes on its own.)
